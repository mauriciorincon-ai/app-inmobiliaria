# ADR 018 — Decide with numbers: a price range, never an appraisal; a payment simulator with its rate and its norm

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-19
- **Sprint:** 010

## Context

Sprint 010 gives a buyer, and an owner, three tools to **decide with numbers**: «Is this listing expensive?», the
data of a zone, and a monthly-payment simulator. Constraints:

- **An asking price is not a closing price, and a cadastral value is land, not a building.** The only price data
  the app has is the asking price of its own active listings. Calling any of it an «appraisal» (_avalúo_) would be
  false and, for a buyer, expensive. The product rule is: **a range, with its N, its date and its source, never a
  single value and never the word _avalúo_.**
- **Every figure and every cap must come from the primary source, verified.** The sprint file said the first
  installment is capped at 30 % of family income (Decreto 145 de 2000, modified by Decreto 3760 de 2008). That is
  **not in force**: Decreto 145 was repealed by art. 2 of Decreto 257 de 2021. Today the cap is in Decreto 1077 de
  2015, art. 2.1.11.1, literal b, as modified by **Decreto 583 de 2025** (28 May 2025, Diario Oficial 53.132): _«La
  primera cuota del crédito no podrá representar más del cuarenta por ciento (40 %) de los ingresos familiares»_,
  for VIS and non-VIS alike. Literal a (unchanged): up to **70 %** of the property value, **80 %** for VIS.
- **A buyer's income is the most sensitive number the app could ever see.** It must never leave the phone.
- **There is not much data at the start.** A range from two listings is noise dressed as a number.
- **The mockup (09, 10) predates the research on the norm.** It uses N ≥ 8 «and neighbours», a 30 % cap and says
  the calculation «is not saved».

## Decision

1. **A range, not a value.** For a listing, take the visible listings of the same operation and type, with an area
   within ±30 %, compute each one's price per m², trim outliers (Tukey fences, 1.5 × IQR), and take the **P25–P75**
   (linear interpolation, type 7), applied to the listing's area. The output always carries the **N**, the date and
   the **source** («asking prices of active listings on Innmobiliaria, not closing prices; not an appraisal»).
2. **N ≥ 5, after trimming, or no range.** With fewer than five comparables there is **no range**, only «not
   enough data». First the listing's barrio; if that does not reach five, the whole localidad, **labelled as such**.
   There is no adjacency data in the barrio catalogue (ADR 014), so «barrio and neighbours» from the mockup is
   replaced by barrio → localidad. (The mockup asked for 8; the product rule of this sprint is 5.)
3. **Four states.** _In range_ (between P25 and P75, or a low price that is not suspicious: «below the range of its
   zone»), _expensive_ (above P75), _suspiciously cheap_ (30 % or more below the **median**, from the buyer
   research), _not enough data_. «Suspiciously cheap» is **shown**, with the advice not to pay any deposit before
   seeing the title; it raises **no flag** yet (operator queue: Sprint 012).
4. **The data of a zone** shows three things, each with its source and date: the **median asking price per m²**
   of the barrio (same trimmed set), the **number of active listings**, and the **cadastral reference value** of the
   **land** per m² (Catastro Bogotá, CC BY 4.0, vigencia 2026). The cadastral number is the median of the medians of
   the blocks (_manzanas_) of the barrio, labelled **«land, by block; not the price of a property»**, and it **never
   enters the range**. The 6 first digits of a block code are the barrio code of ADR 014 (99 % match in a 2 000-block
   sample; 983 of the 1 000 urban barrios have a value). It is generated by `scripts/valor-referencia/` (raw data
   downloaded by hand and not versioned; deterministic output, ~23 KB) and imported **only on the server**.
5. **The rate comes with its date.** The daily cron reads one aggregated query of the open dataset `qzsc-9esp`
   (Superintendencia Financiera, weekly, **CC BY-SA 4.0**): «Adquisición de vivienda no VIS (colocación en pesos)»,
   fixed rate, **weighted by the amount disbursed**, for the latest `fecha_corte`; it stores it in `tasas_vivienda`
   with its date. If the read fails, the app uses the last stored rate; if there is none, a rate written in the
   repo **with its own date**. The simulator always says «rate of <date>, Superfinanciera», lets the buyer write
   their own, and warns when the rate is more than 30 days old. The read has an 8 s timeout and a failure never
   fails the cron. Share-alike applies to the dataset's derivatives; the single number we publish carries the
   attribution.
6. **The simulator is «a calculation, not an approval».** Fixed monthly payment in pesos (French amortisation)
   from the effective annual rate: monthly rate = (1 + EA)^(1/12) − 1. **Capacity** = income × **40 %**; the loan is
   at most **70 %** of the price (the user may enter a larger down payment); **maximum price** = maximum loan ÷ (1 −
   down-payment share). The norm (Decreto 583 de 2025) is shown next to the figure. VIS (80 %), UVR, leasing and
   subsidies are out of scope; the screen mentions VIS as a note.
7. **The calculation stays on the phone, and only a maximum price travels.** The result is saved in `localStorage`
   (`inn:capacidad:v1`, like favorites): the mockup said «it is not saved», the product now says «it does **not
   leave your phone**». It filters `/buscar` through `max=<maximum price>` (a price, not an income), shows on each
   listing «≈ $X a month · fits / does not fit your capacity», and prefills the maximum price of a new alert. **No
   request ever carries the income**; an e2e intercepts every request and looks for it. *Known limit (noted in S16):*
   the maximum price is derived from the income, so whoever sees a `/buscar?max=…` URL (in the server's access log, or a
   link the person shares by hand) can infer its order of magnitude. The app never shares that URL on its own.
8. **Reads, not writes.** Migration 14 is additive: the `tasas_vivienda` table (closed, RLS without policies),
   `tasa_vivienda()` (public: an open-data rate is not personal data), `guardar_tasa_vivienda()` (server only) and
   `comparables_precio(operation, type, localidad)` (public: reuses `inmuebles_que_cumplen`, the same visibility
   `where` as `/buscar`, and returns only slug, sector code, area and price, up to 1 000, `[]` if any argument is
   null). No existing function changes. The range itself is computed by a pure engine, not in SQL.

## Consequences

- The buyer sees how an asking price compares with its zone, with the evidence behind it, and an owner sees the
  same range when publishing: price becomes a conversation about data for both.
- **The range is only as good as the catalogue.** At the start there will be many barrios with «not enough
  data»; the fall back to the localidad is labelled, and the owner can later bring the year's data as a source (the
  engine receives its source as data).
- The 40 %/70 % caps are **constants with their citation** in one file; the day the norm changes, one edit and one
  test change. The sprint file's 30 % is recorded as a deviation.
- One more subrequest a day in the cron (the Workers free plan has a low ceiling; recorded with the digest debt of
  ADR 017).
- **Out of scope:** the paperwork assistant and the title reader (S11); reporting, anti-fraud flags and the
  operator queue (S12); UVR, leasing, subsidies; any single-value «appraisal».
- **Licences:** cadastral reference value, CC BY 4.0 («Catastro Bogotá»); interest rates, CC BY-SA 4.0
  («Superintendencia Financiera de Colombia»). Both are shown next to the figure that uses them.
